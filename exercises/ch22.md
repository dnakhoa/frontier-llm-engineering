## 22.1 🔢 ★ The decode floor

A 70B model in BF16 on a single H100 (3.35 TB/s HBM, 990 TFLOP/s BF16).

1. How long to read all the weights once?
2. At batch size 1, what is the minimum time per output token, and what is the tensor-core utilization?
3. At what batch size does decode become compute-bound? Show the arithmetic-intensity calculation.
4. At batch 256, what is the time per token, and what limits it now?
5. Why does quantizing the weights to FP8 help decode but not prefill?

## 22.2 🔢 ★★ KV cache and concurrency

A 70B model, 80 layers, head dim 128, BF16, served on 2× H100 with TP=2.

1. KV cache per token under MHA (64 KV heads), GQA 8:1, and MQA.
2. Model weights per GPU. How much HBM is left for cache?
3. Under GQA 8:1, how many concurrent 8K-context requests fit?
4. How many concurrent 128K-context requests?
5. You quantize the KV cache to FP8. Recompute part 3 and part 4.
6. Which single change buys the most concurrency, and what does it cost?

## 22.3 🔢 ★★ Batching and fragmentation

Your traffic has prompt lengths averaging 1,200 tokens and output lengths distributed as: 60% at 100 tokens, 30% at 500, 10% at 4,000.

1. Under static batching with 32 slots, what fraction of slot-steps are wasted?
2. Under continuous batching, what changes?
3. Under naive KV allocation reserving max length (8K) per slot, how much cache is reserved versus used?
4. With paged attention and 16-token blocks, what is the internal fragmentation?
5. Which of the two — continuous batching or paging — would you implement first, and why?

## 22.4 🏗️ ★★ Serve a reasoning model

You must serve a model whose outputs average 8,000 tokens with a heavy tail to 60,000, on 8× H100.

1. Which of Chapter 22's assumptions break, and how?
2. Design the batching policy. What do you do about the tail?
3. What does best-of-$n$ at $n = 8$ do to your memory, and what feature saves you?
4. Most of the reasoning tokens are never shown to the user. How does that change your throughput metric?
5. Would you disaggregate prefill and decode here? Argue both.

## 22.5 🔢 ★★ Speculative decoding

A 70B target and a 1B draft. Draft generates $k = 4$ tokens sequentially; the target verifies all 4 in one forward pass.

1. Time for the draft's 4 tokens, assuming the draft is 70× cheaper per token.
2. Time for one target forward pass at batch 1.
3. If the acceptance rate is 0.7 per token, what is the expected number of accepted tokens per cycle?
4. What is the speedup versus plain decoding?
5. At batch 256, redo the analysis. What happened, and why?

## 22.6 🏗️ ★★ Diagnose eight serving symptoms

For each, give the two most likely causes and the first check.

1. Throughput is 8× below a colleague's on identical hardware.
2. OOM at 40 concurrent requests, fine at 30, model weights use only half the HBM.
3. p50 TTFT is 180 ms; p99 is 9 s.
4. INT4 quantization made prefill 20% slower.
5. Speculative decoding is slower than plain decoding under load.
6. The model's benchmark scores dropped 3 points after moving to the serving stack.
7. Throughput collapses on some batches and not others, on an MoE model.
8. TPOT rises smoothly with the number of concurrent requests.

## 22.7 🔢 ★★ Goodput

Your SLO is p95 TTFT ≤ 500 ms and p95 TPOT ≤ 50 ms.

| Max batch | Throughput (tok/s) | p95 TTFT | p95 TPOT |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | 4,200 | 210 ms | 22 ms |
| 32 | 7,100 | 340 ms | 31 ms |
| 64 | 10,800 | 620 ms | 44 ms |
| 128 | 14,200 | 1,400 ms | 71 ms |

1. Which configurations meet the SLO?
2. What is the goodput-maximizing choice?
3. Batch 64 fails only on TTFT. What technique would fix that specifically, and what does it cost?
4. After that fix, re-evaluate batch 64. What would you need to measure?
5. Why is reporting the batch-128 number as "our throughput" misleading?
