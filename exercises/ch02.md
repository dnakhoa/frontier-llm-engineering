# Exercises — Chapter 2: The org chart

Covers [Chapter 2](../book/part-1-frontier/02-org-chart-and-roles.md). Solutions: [`solutions/ch02.md`](solutions/ch02.md).

Legend: 🔢 drill · 🏗️ design · 📄 paper · ★ direct · ★★ synthesis · ★★★ beyond the chapter

This set has less arithmetic than most. It is about developing a map of who does what — which is what makes the rest of the book navigable, and what makes a JD readable.

---

## 2.1 🏗️ ★ Route the incident

For each incident, name the role that owns the *diagnosis* and the role that owns the *fix*. They are often different, and saying why is the exercise.

1. Training throughput dropped 30% overnight with no code change.
2. The loss spiked at step 40,000 and did not recover after a rollback.
3. The model's MMLU score dropped four points after post-training.
4. A benchmark the model scores well on turns out to appear in the training corpus.
5. p99 serving latency tripled after a model update, with p50 unchanged.
6. The MoE all-to-all is taking 40% of step time.
7. The model refuses a benign request in Vietnamese but answers it in English.
8. A run resumed successfully after a node failure, but the loss is now suspiciously low.

## 2.2 🏗️ ★★ Staff the run

You are starting a lab. Goal: train a 30B MoE from scratch and ship it as a chat product within 12 months. You have budget for 18 engineers and researchers.

1. Allocate the 18 headcount across the roles in §2.2. Justify every number above 2 and every role you set to 0.
2. Which three hires do you make *first*, and why those three?
3. Which role would you most like to have and cannot afford at 18 people? What breaks as a result, and how do you mitigate it?
4. Now the budget drops to 9. What do you cut, and what do you stop trying to do?

## 2.3 🏗️ ★★ The handoff surfaces

Chapter 2 §2.5 covers how teams interact. For each pair, name the artifact that passes between them and the most common way the handoff fails.

1. Pre-training Data Engineer → Pre-training Researcher
2. Pre-training Researcher → Large-Scale Training Engineer
3. Large-Scale Training Engineer → Post-training Researcher
4. Post-training Researcher → Evaluation Engineer
5. Post-training Engineer → Inference Engineer
6. Kernel Engineer → everyone

For each, say what a well-designed interface between those two roles would specify.

## 2.4 🏗️ ★★ Where the boundary should sit

§2.4 notes the boundaries between roles are blurry. For each pair, argue where the line should be drawn, and name the failure mode of drawing it in the wrong place.

1. Pre-training Researcher vs Large-Scale Training Engineer, on the parallelism configuration.
2. Post-training Researcher vs Data Engineer, on the SFT mixture.
3. RL Engineer vs Inference Engineer, on the rollout generation stack.
4. Evaluation Engineer vs everyone optimizing against evaluation.

Item 4 is the one that matters most; Chapters 12, 13, and 23 all make the same structural argument about it.

## 2.5 📄 ★★ Reverse-engineer a team

Pick one published technical report — DeepSeek-V3 [\[1\]](../book/appendix/b-references.md#1-deepseek-v3), Llama 3 [\[5\]](../book/appendix/b-references.md#5-llama-3), or Qwen3 [\[6\]](../book/appendix/b-references.md#6-qwen3) — and read its contributions and acknowledgements sections.

1. Which of §2.2's roles can you infer existed, from the work described?
2. Which sections of the report were clearly written by an infrastructure person rather than a researcher? What gives it away?
3. Roughly how many people worked on it, and what does the author list tell you about how the team was structured?
4. What does the *balance* of the report — how many pages on data versus architecture versus infrastructure versus post-training — tell you about where that lab's effort went?

## 2.6 🏗️ ★★★ Your own map

1. Place yourself on §2.2's taxonomy. Which role are you closest to today, given your actual current skills rather than your interests?
2. Which role do you want in three years?
3. List the specific gaps between them, mapped to chapters of this book.
4. For each gap, name a concrete artifact you could produce in a month that would be evidence you have closed it. ([Chapter 24 §24.6](../book/part-5-the-job/24-translating-jds.md) has the menu.)
5. Which single gap, if closed, unlocks the most others?

Keep the answer. [Chapter 25's exercise set](ch25.md) asks you to revise it with the career map in hand.
