## 16.1 🔢 ★ Correct by luck

A model solves math problems in 6 steps. Each step is independently correct with probability 0.85. An error in one step is compensated by a later error, producing the right final answer, 8% of the time when there are exactly two errors.

1. Probability all 6 steps are correct.
2. Probability of exactly two errors.
3. Probability of reaching the right answer with exactly two errors.
4. Of all responses that reach the right answer, roughly what fraction had a flawed derivation?
5. Under outcome-only GRPO, what happens to the flawed ones?

## 16.3 🔢 ★★ Search-time scaling

A model has 40% pass@1 on a benchmark. Assume independence across samples.

1. Expected pass@$n$ for $n = 4, 16, 64, 256$.
2. Majority voting requires the *correct* answer to be the mode. If wrong answers are spread across 8 distinct values uniformly, estimate the majority-vote accuracy at $n = 16$.
3. A perfect verifier would achieve pass@$n$. Your verifier picks correctly among the $n$ candidates 80% of the time when a correct one is present. What accuracy does best-of-$n$ achieve at $n = 16$?
4. Why does the independence assumption break in practice, and in which direction?
