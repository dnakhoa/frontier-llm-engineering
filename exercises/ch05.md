## 5.1 🔢 ★ Count the parameters

Llama-3-70B: 80 layers, $d = 8192$, 64 query heads, 8 KV heads, head dim 128, SwiGLU with $d_{\text{ff}} = 28672$, vocabulary 128,256, untied embeddings.

1. Parameters in one attention block (Q, K, V, and output projections).
2. Parameters in one SwiGLU FFN block. (Remember SwiGLU has *three* matrices, not two.)
3. Parameters in all 80 layers, including RMSNorm.
4. Total including both embedding tables. How close is it to 70B?
5. What fraction of the model is attention versus FFN?

## 5.2 🔢 ★★ KV cache across four attention variants

Same model shape — 80 layers, 64 query heads, head dim 128, BF16 — under four attention schemes:

| Scheme | KV heads |
|---|---|
| MHA | 64 |
| GQA 8:1 | 8 |
| MQA | 1 |
| MLA | latent $d_c = 512$ plus decoupled RoPE key $d_h^R = 64$: 576 numbers per layer per token, as in DeepSeek-V3 |

1. KV cache bytes per token for each.
2. Cache for one 128K-context sequence, each.
3. On an 80 GB H100 with the 140 GB of weights spread over two GPUs (so 10 GB free per GPU for cache), how many 8K-context sequences fit under each scheme?
4. Rank the four by quality, and explain why the field settled on GQA rather than MQA.

## 5.3 🔢 ★★ The MoE bargain

DeepSeek-V3: 671B total, 37B active, 61 layers of which the first 3 are dense and 58 are MoE, $d = 7168$, 256 routed experts + 1 shared, top-8 routing, expert $d_{\text{ff}} = 2048$ ([fact sheet](../book/appendix/fact-sheets/deepseek-v3.md#architecture)).

1. Parameters in one expert.
2. Parameters in all routed experts across all layers. What fraction of the 671B is that?
3. Per token, how many expert parameters are active? Does that reconcile with 37B active total?
4. Compare the training FLOPs of DeepSeek-V3 against a hypothetical dense 671B model on the same tokens. What is the ratio?
5. Compare the *memory* required to train each. What is that ratio?
6. State the MoE bargain in one sentence, using your answers to 4 and 5.

## 5.4 🔢 ★★ Fine-grained versus coarse experts

DeepSeek-V3 uses 256 routed experts (plus 1 shared) of $d_{\text{ff}} = 2048$ at $d = 7168$, with top-8 routing. Mixtral 8×7B uses 8 experts of $d_{\text{ff}} = 14336$ at $d = 4096$, with top-2. Qwen3-235B-A22B has 94 layers at $d = 4096$, 128 experts, top-8, and no shared expert. Its `config.json` has two FFN widths: `intermediate_size` = 12,288 and `moe_intermediate_size` = 1,536 (values on the [Qwen3 fact sheet](../book/appendix/fact-sheets/qwen3.md#architecture-qwen3-235b-a22b)).

1. Which of Qwen3's two widths is the expert width? Decide by arithmetic, not by the field name. For each candidate, compute the active expert parameters per token per layer, multiply by 94, and add attention (about 71M per layer) and the untied embeddings ($2 \times 151{,}936 \times 4096$). Which candidate reproduces the published 22B active?
2. With the right width, how many expert parameters are active per token per layer in each of the three models?
3. How many distinct combinations of experts can each model select per token?
4. Why is Qwen3 fine-grained at 1,536? Multiply its expert width by its top-$k$: where else in the config does that number appear? Use it to say what fine-grained expert segmentation holds fixed and what it multiplies. Then express each model's expert width as a fraction of its $d$ and order the three from fine to coarse.
5. Argue for fine-grained experts (many small) over coarse (few large). Then argue the other side.
6. What does fine-grained routing cost in the all-to-all communication of §6.8?

## 5.5 🔢 ★★ RoPE and the frequency budget

RoPE with base $\theta$ assigns rotation frequency $\theta^{-2i/d}$ to dimension pair $i$.

1. For head dim 128 and $\theta = 10{,}000$, what is the wavelength of the *slowest*-rotating dimension pair, in tokens?
2. Llama 3 uses $\theta = 500{,}000$. What is the slowest wavelength now?
3. Why does raising $\theta$ help long-context extrapolation? Answer in terms of what the model has and has not seen.
4. A model trained at 8K with $\theta = 10{,}000$ is evaluated at 32K. Which dimension pairs are out of distribution, and which are fine?

## 5.7 🔢 ★★ Load balance, and what breaks without it

You have 64 experts and top-2 routing over a batch of 8,192 tokens.

1. Under perfect balance, how many tokens does each expert receive?
2. Expert parallelism places 8 experts per GPU across 8 GPUs. Under perfect balance, how many tokens per GPU?
3. One expert receives 12% of all routed tokens instead of its 1.6% share. What is the step-time impact, given that the all-to-all is a synchronous collective?
4. Chapter 5 §5.10 describes DeepSeek's auxiliary-loss-*free* balancing. What is the mechanism, and what does it avoid that an auxiliary loss does not?
