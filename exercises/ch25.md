# Exercises — Chapter 25: Career paths

Covers [Chapter 25](../book/part-5-the-job/25-career-paths.md). Solutions: [`solutions/ch25.md`](solutions/ch25.md).

Legend: 🏗️ design · 📄 paper · ★ direct · ★★ synthesis · ★★★ beyond the chapter

This set is personal. The solutions give the reasoning, not the answer.

---

## 25.1 🏗️ ★★ Revise your map

Return to [Exercise 2.6](ch02.md), where you placed yourself on the role taxonomy and named your target.

1. With §25.1's scarcity column in hand, does your target change?
2. Which scarce role is closest to your current skills?
3. Would entering through that role and transitioning internally be faster than a direct move?
4. What would you give up by taking that path, and is it recoverable?

---

## 25.2 🏗️ ★★ The PhD question, for you specifically

1. Is your target role in §25.2's "genuinely helps" list or the "does not matter" list?
2. If it is in the first list, what is the underlying skill the PhD is evidence *of*?
3. Can you demonstrate that skill another way? Be concrete about the artifact.
4. Estimate the opportunity cost of a PhD in your situation, in years and in forgone experience.
5. §25.2 notes the techniques you would specialize in will likely be superseded. What would *not* be?

---

## 25.3 🏗️ ★★ Design the transition

1. State your current role and your target, from [Exercise 24.6](ch24.md).
2. Which of §25.4's transitions does yours resemble — easy, harder, or the narrow door?
3. If it is a hard transition, what is the intermediate step?
4. Build a 12-month plan with a deliverable every quarter.
5. What is the first thing you would do this week?

---

## 25.4 🏗️ ★★ Where to work

For each of your top two target employers, answer:

1. Which category from §25.6 is it — large frontier lab, small lab, applied team, open source, cloud/hardware vendor?
2. What slice of the pipeline would you actually touch there?
3. What would you learn there that you could not learn at the other?
4. What would you *not* learn?
5. Which one better serves the role you want in five years, as opposed to the one you want next?

---

## 25.5 📄 ★★ Read across the divide

§25.7 argues that a meaningful fraction of the field's published technical detail comes from Chinese labs, and that filtering it out means working with less information.

1. Name the four technical reports this book leans on most heavily and where each came from.
2. Pick one you have not read and read its infrastructure or post-training section.
3. What did it tell you that a Western lab's report of the same period did not?
4. What open-weight models are you or your team already using, and where were they trained?

---

## 25.6 🏗️ ★★★ What compounds

§25.8 distinguishes what compounds from what does not.

1. List five specific things you have learned in the last two years. For each, will it still be valuable in five years?
2. §25.8 says PPO expertise transferred to GRPO through the *shared structure*, not the implementation. Give another example of that pattern from this book.
3. Name a skill you have that is framework-specific and estimate its half-life.
4. What are you currently spending time on that will not compound? What would you do instead?
5. Write the one sentence of career advice you would give someone starting this book today.
