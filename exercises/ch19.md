## 19.1 🏗️ ★★★ Reconstruct the pipeline

Without looking at Chapter 19, write out the full R1 pipeline.

1. The starting model, and two properties of it that matter downstream.
2. R1-Zero: the recipe, the reward, and the result.
3. What R1-Zero established, and what it broke.
4. All four stages of R1 proper, with what each stage is *for*.
5. The distillation result and its implication.
6. The two abandoned approaches and why.

Then check. Which of the six did you miss, and which chapter should you reread?

## 19.2 🔢 ★★ The two AIME numbers

R1-Zero is reported at 15.6% → 71.0% pass@1, and 86.7% with majority voting at 64 samples.

1. If samples were independent at 71%, what would pass@64 be? Why is the reported 86.7% so much lower than that?
2. What does the gap between pass@64 (implied) and majority-vote-64 tell you?
3. At 3,000 tokens per sample, what is the token cost per query for each number?
4. Write the one-line honest statement of R1-Zero's AIME result.

## 19.3 🏗️ ★★ What each stage is for

For each of R1's four stages, answer: what problem does it solve, what would break without it, and could it be merged with an adjacent stage?

1. Cold-start SFT.
2. Reasoning RL.
3. Rejection sampling + second SFT.
4. Final RL.

Then: R1-Zero skipped stages 1, 3, and 4 entirely and still worked. What does that tell you about which stages are about *capability* and which about *product*?

## 19.4 🔢 ★★ The language-consistency trade

R1 added a language-consistency reward and reported it slightly reduced accuracy.

1. Why would restricting the model to one language reduce reasoning accuracy? Give a mechanism.
2. This is a capability tax ([Chapter 11 §11.9](../book/part-3-post-training/11-sft-frontier-style.md)). Name three others in this book and what each trades.
3. Suppose it costs 1.5 points of AIME accuracy. Argue for keeping it.
4. Now suppose it costs 8 points. Does your answer change? What would you do instead?
5. What does the fact that they *reported* this tell you about the report?

## 19.5 🔢 ★★ Distillation versus RL on a small model

You have a 7B base model and a compute budget of 50,000 GPU-hours.

1. Option A: run GRPO on the 7B directly. Estimate what you can afford, using [Exercise 13.7](ch13.md)'s generation costs.
2. Option B: SFT the 7B on 800k traces distilled from an existing strong reasoner. Estimate the cost.
3. Which does R1's reported result favour, and by how much of a margin?
4. Give the mechanistic explanation, referencing §19.4's finding about latent capability.
5. Under what condition would Option A win?

## 19.7 🏗️ ★★★ Reproduce it at 1/100 scale

You have a strong 7B base model and 8 H100s for two weeks.

1. Which of R1's stages can you actually run? Which cannot you, and why?
2. Design your prompt set: source, size, and difficulty calibration.
3. Write your reward function.
4. What do you expect to reproduce, and what do you expect to fail to reproduce?
5. What is the single measurement that would tell you it worked?

## 19.8 🏗️ ★★ Where the moat is

§19.8 and §19.10 both conclude that the data, not the algorithm, is the advantage.

1. State the evidence for that claim from three separate reports in this book.
2. If true, what does it imply about which roles a frontier lab is short of? (Cross-reference [Chapter 25 §25.1](../book/part-5-the-job/25-career-paths.md).)
