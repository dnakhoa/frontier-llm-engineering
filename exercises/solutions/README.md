# Solutions

Worked solutions to every [exercise set](../README.md).

## Read this first

**Write your answer before you open the solution.** This is not moralizing; it is the mechanism. Recognizing a correct answer costs you nothing and teaches you nothing. Producing one, being wrong, and seeing exactly where you went wrong is the entire value of a problem set.

## How the solutions are written

**Drills (🔢)** show the full calculation, including the intermediate steps and the unit conversions, because unit errors are where these actually go wrong. Where a rounding convention matters (GB vs GiB, 1e9 vs 2^30) the solution states which one it used and why the distinction does or does not matter here.

**Design problems (🏗️)** do not give "the answer." They give a defensible answer, the reasoning that produced it, the two or three alternatives that were seriously considered, and the conditions under which each alternative would win. If your answer differs but your reasoning covers the same constraints, you got it right. If your answer matches but you did not consider the constraints, you got it wrong for the right-looking reasons, which is worse.

**Paper questions (📄)** quote the relevant passage, cite the section, and explain what it means. Where the paper is ambiguous — which happens more than you would hope — the solution says so rather than manufacturing certainty.

**Lab prompts (🧪)** give the expected outcome and, more usefully, explain *why* the common wrong prediction is wrong.

## When a solution is wrong

It happens. Solutions are checked but not infallible, and some of them depend on figures that a newer technical report may have superseded.

If you are confident a solution is wrong, [open an issue](../../CONTRIBUTING.md) with the exercise number and your reasoning. Corrections to solutions are the second-most-valuable contribution to this book, after corrections to citations.
