## 15.1 🔢 ★ Zero-variance groups

Your prompt set has these measured pass rates under the current policy:

| Bucket | Pass rate | Share of prompts |
|---|---|---|
| Easy | 0.95 | 30% |
| Medium | 0.50 | 20% |
| Hard | 0.10 | 30% |
| Impossible | 0.00 | 20% |

With $G = 8$ samples per prompt and a binary reward:

1. For each bucket, the probability that all 8 samples give the same reward.
2. What fraction of prompts contribute *zero* gradient?
3. What is your effective batch size as a fraction of your nominal one?
4. Repeat with $G = 32$. How much does it help, and at what cost?
5. Design a filtering rule that would double your effective batch size, and state what it costs you.

## 15.2 🔢 ★★ Group size economics

3. Plot — or tabulate — useful gradient per second of generation for each $G$.
4. Which $G$ do you choose, and what changes your answer?

## 15.3 🔢 ★★ The Dr. GRPO biases

Chapter 15 §15.7 and §15.9 describe two biases in the standard GRPO objective.

1. Write the per-token gradient weight under the $1/|o_i|$ length normalization, for a response of 500 tokens and one of 5,000 tokens with the same advantage.
2. For a *wrong* answer (negative advantage), which response is penalized more per token? What behaviour does that select for?
3. A prompt has rewards `[1,1,1,1,1,1,1,0]`; another has `[1,0,1,0,1,0,1,0]`. Compute the group std for each.
4. After dividing by std, which prompt's advantages are larger in magnitude? Is that the prompt you want to learn from?
5. State both Dr. GRPO fixes and what each costs.

## 15.5 🏗️ ★★ Diagnose the three GRPO failures

1. Accuracy plateaus at step 3,000 while mean response length keeps rising from 2,000 to 9,000 tokens.
2. Format compliance hits 99.8% by step 400; accuracy is flat at its step-0 value.
3. Traces begin containing fragments in a second language, and readability degrades. Accuracy is fine.
4. For each: is this reward hacking in the [Chapter 12 §12.6](../book/part-3-post-training/12-reward-modeling.md) sense? Explain the distinction carefully.
