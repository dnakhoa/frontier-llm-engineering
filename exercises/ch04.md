## 4.1 🔢 ★ The embedding tax

A model has hidden size $d = 8{,}192$ and 80 layers. You are choosing between vocabulary sizes of 32K, 128K, and 256K.

1. How many parameters does the embedding table hold at each size?
2. If the output projection is untied from the input embedding, how many total parameters do the two tables hold?
3. The non-embedding parameters are roughly 65B. What fraction of the model is embeddings at each vocabulary size?
4. At which vocabulary size does the embedding table exceed 10% of the model, and why does that matter beyond the parameter count?

## 4.2 🔢 ★★ Fertility and the compute bill

Fertility is tokens per word. Your tokenizer measures:

| Language | Fertility |
|---|---|
| English | 1.30 |
| Vietnamese | 2.45 |
| Japanese | 1.85 |
| Python | 1.60 |

1. A 1,000-word document in each language costs how many tokens?
2. Training compute is $6ND$ where $D$ is tokens. If your corpus is 40% English, 20% Vietnamese, 20% Japanese, 20% code *by word count*, what fraction of your compute goes to each language by **token** count?
3. You improve Vietnamese fertility from 2.45 to 1.60 by growing the vocabulary. What fraction of total compute does that free up?
4. A Vietnamese user sends a 500-word prompt to your API, which bills per token. How much more do they pay than an English user sending the same content? Is that defensible?

## 4.3 🔢 ★★ Context length is a tokenizer property

Your model has a 128K-token context window.

1. Using the fertility table above, how many *words* of each language fit in the context?
2. A Vietnamese legal document of 60,000 words — does it fit?
3. Your product promises "fits a 300-page book." At 250 words per page, does that hold for all four languages?
4. State in one sentence why "128K context" is a misleading product claim, and what you would report instead.

## 4.4 🏗️ ★★ Choose a vocabulary

You are training a 30B model for English, Vietnamese, Japanese, and code. Choose the vocabulary size and justify it against every trade-off in §4.4.

1. Your choice, with a number.
2. The argument from fertility.
3. The argument from embedding parameters.
4. The argument from the softmax cost at the output layer.
5. The argument from rare-token undertraining — with a 6T-token corpus, roughly how many times does the *least* frequent token in a 256K vocabulary appear?
6. The storage argument from [Exercise 3.8](ch03.md) — does `uint16` matter to you?
7. What would you have to believe for a 32K vocabulary to be the right answer instead?

## 4.5 🔢 ★★ Why you cannot re-tokenize

You have a fully pre-trained 30B model and you discover the tokenizer handles Vietnamese diacritics badly.

1. Why can you not simply swap in a better tokenizer and keep the weights?
2. Estimate the cost of retraining from scratch, using [Exercise 1.2](ch01.md)'s method with 6T tokens.
3. Name three partial mitigations that do not require a full retrain, and state what each costs in quality.
4. What check, taking under a day, would have caught this before the run started?

## 4.6 🏗️ ★★ Special tokens, planned properly

Design the special-token set for a model you intend to support for three years, covering chat, tool calling, reasoning traces, and fill-in-the-middle code completion.

1. List every special token, with its purpose.
2. How many reserved/unused tokens do you allocate, and why? (See [Ch 11 Exercise 11.10](ch11.md) part 5.)

## 4.9 🔢 ★★★ The rare-token problem

Your vocabulary has 128K tokens and your corpus is 6T tokens. Token frequencies follow roughly a Zipf distribution.

1. Under Zipf's law with exponent 1, what fraction of all token occurrences do the top 1,000 tokens account for?
2. Roughly how many occurrences does the 100,000th-ranked token get?
3. Is that enough to learn a useful embedding? What is your reasoning?
4. What does this imply about the *marginal* value of vocabulary tokens 128K through 256K?
5. Chapter 4 notes you cannot re-tokenize a trained model. Given part 4, what is the argument for making the vocabulary *larger* than fertility alone would justify?
