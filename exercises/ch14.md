## 14.1 🔢 ★★ Derive it yourself

Without looking at §14.2, derive the DPO loss from the KL-constrained RLHF objective.

1. Write the objective and state the closed-form optimal policy.
2. Invert it to express the reward in terms of the policy.
3. Substitute into Bradley-Terry and show the partition function cancels. State precisely *why* it cancels.
4. Write the final loss.
5. What would break if Bradley-Terry depended on absolute rewards rather than differences?

## 14.4 🔢 ★★ The decreasing-logprob phenomenon

Over 500 DPO steps you observe: `chosen_reward` −0.02 → −0.85, `rejected_reward` −0.03 → −1.94, accuracy 51% → 89%.

1. What is the margin at the start and at the end?
2. Is the loss decreasing? Is accuracy improving?
3. What has happened to the absolute probability of the chosen responses?
4. Where did the probability mass go?
5. Name two failure modes this predicts, and the metric that would confirm each.
6. Which variant in §14.7 is specifically designed to prevent this, and how?

## 14.6 🏗️ ★★ On-policy or off

You have three preference datasets for a model you are about to DPO:

- **A:** pairs sampled from your current SFT model, labelled by humans.
- **B:** a public dataset of pairs from a mix of open models, AI-labelled.
- **C:** pairs where both responses came from a much stronger closed model.

1. Rank them for training your model, and justify.
2. For dataset C specifically, describe what DPO actually does to your model's distribution.
3. You have a small budget of A and a large amount of B. Design the mixture.
4. Design an iterative loop that converts your budget into on-policy data over three rounds.
5. How would you *measure* whether off-policy data is hurting you?

## 14.7 🏗️ ★★ Pick a variant

For each situation, choose among DPO, IPO, KTO, ORPO, and SimPO, in one or two sentences each.

1. Your only feedback data is thumbs-up/thumbs-down from production.
2. Your DPO runs produce responses that grow 60% longer with no quality gain.
3. Your preferences are near-deterministic (annotators agree 95%) and DPO overfits fast.
4. You have no SFT stage and want one pipeline from base model to aligned.
5. You are memory-constrained and cannot hold or precompute a reference.
6. You want the best-understood, most-debugged option and have no unusual constraint.
