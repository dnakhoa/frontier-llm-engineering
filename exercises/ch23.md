## 23.1 🔢 ★★ The confidence interval that invalidates the result

Your model scores 67.1% on HumanEval (164 problems); a competitor scores 64.6%.

1. Compute the standard error of each estimate, treating each problem as a Bernoulli trial.
2. What is the 95% confidence interval on each score?
3. What is the standard error of the *difference*, and is the 2.5-point gap significant?
4. How many problems would you need for a 2.5-point difference to be significant at 95%?
5. Both numbers appear in press releases. What should each have reported?

## 23.2 🔢 ★★ Prompt sensitivity

You evaluate the same model on MMLU five ways: option labels `A)` vs `(A)` vs `1.`; scoring by generation vs by option log-probability; 0-shot vs 5-shot. Scores range from 71.2% to 78.9%.

1. What is the spread, and how does it compare to typical reported differences between frontier models?
2. Which of the three variables would you expect to matter most, and why?
3. A paper reports 78.9% without stating the harness. What has it told you?
4. Write the reporting standard that makes the number reproducible.
5. Your team tries six prompt formats and reports the best. What have you measured?

## 23.3 🏗️ ★★ Detect contamination

You suspect GSM8K is in your 14T-token corpus.

1. Design an n-gram check. What $n$, and what exactly do you compare?
2. Your check returns 0.3% overlap. What can and cannot you conclude?
3. Name four contamination routes an n-gram check misses entirely.
4. Design a check that catches paraphrased contamination. What does it cost?
5. Your SFT data was generated by a model that may have seen GSM8K. What check catches that?
6. What would you do if you found 40% overlap two weeks before launch?

## 23.4 🏗️ ★★ Correct a judge

You use a strong model to compare your new checkpoint against the previous one on 2,000 prompts. Raw result: 61% win rate for the new model.

1. Name three biases affecting this number and the direction of each.
2. Design the position-bias correction. What does it cost?
3. Mean response lengths are 410 (old) and 620 (new). What must you report, and what additional measurement isolates quality from length?
4. The judge is the same model family as your policy. What does that do to the number?
5. After all corrections, the win rate is 53%. Is the new model better? What would you do next?

Design the internal evaluation suite for a 30B general assistant, with a 12-month roadmap of post-training experiments.

## 23.6 🔢 ★★ Report a reasoning model

Your model scores: MATH-500 pass@1 = 71%, maj@32 = 84%, maj@256 = 87%. Mean 4,200 tokens per sample.

1. Tokens and cost per query at each setting, at $2/M output tokens.
2. Plot — or tabulate — the accuracy-versus-compute curve. Where does it flatten?
3. Which number belongs in a model card, and in what form?
4. A competitor reports 86% without stating a budget. What can you conclude about their model?
5. Design the reporting format you would want the whole field to adopt.

## 23.8 🧪 ★★ Break the contamination lab

Run `lab23_contamination_check`.

1. Plant verbatim benchmark items in a corpus and run the n-gram detector. Predict the recall.
2. Paraphrase the planted items and re-run. Predict the recall now.

4. Translate the planted items into another language. Predict what each detector finds.
