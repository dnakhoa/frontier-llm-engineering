# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Argue about the work, not the person. Assume the other reader is smart and is missing a piece of context, not that they are stupid. Nobody here is being paid, and everyone is learning something hard.

## What is expected

- **Be specific.** "Section 6.7 is wrong" is not useful. "Section 6.7 says ring attention is O(1) memory per device; the Liu et al. paper says O(N/P), and here is the line" is useful.
- **Be generous about background.** This book has readers who train models for a living and readers who have never seen a cluster. Both are welcome. Answer the question that was asked.
- **Cite when you correct.** A correction without a source is an opinion.
- **Accept correction the same way.** Being wrong in public about a technical detail costs nothing. Defending a wrong detail costs the book its credibility.

## What is not acceptable

- Personal attacks, harassment, or demeaning comments — about anyone, in issues, pull requests, or commit messages.
- Sexualized language or imagery, and unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Publishing others' private information without explicit permission.
- Sustained disruption: reopening settled arguments, brigading, or derailing threads.
- Gatekeeping. "You should already know this" is never a useful contribution to a book whose entire premise is that this knowledge should be freely available.

## Scope

This applies in every project space — issues, pull requests, discussions, and any communication that represents the project in public.

## Enforcement

Report problems by opening an issue, or privately to the maintainers if the issue itself would be inappropriate to make public. Reports are handled confidentially.

Maintainers may edit or remove comments, commits, issues, and pull requests that violate this document, and may temporarily or permanently ban contributors for behaviour they consider inappropriate, threatening, or harmful.
